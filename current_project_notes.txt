## 1. Implementation Progress
- Single cyberneuron matches the 2009 paper: per-input substitution tables, signed-byte sum, four confidence bands, sequential/random ±1 teaching, and `learningDivider` (`k = 1 / divider`).
- `CyberNeuron` hardened and fixed:
  - Fixed `lowerEdge(MAY_BE_NO)` from `25` (`THRESHOLD_NO`, which belongs to `NO`) to `26` (`THRESHOLD_NO + 1`), eliminating the `IllegalStateException` crash when teaching `MAY_BE_NO` at `sum == 25` and ensuring `divider = 1` enters `MAY_BE_NO`.
  - Fixed `signedGap` for multi-row sums outside `-128..127` (`lowerEdge(NO) = Integer.MIN_VALUE`, `upperEdge(YES) = Integer.MAX_VALUE`, plus immediate in-band zero return and saturating `long` subtraction).
  - Validated `inputSize`, `maxInputValue`, integer table-size overflow, negative `offset` in `calc(int, int[])`, and `null`/bounds arguments across constructors and mutators without SpotBugs `CT_CONSTRUCTOR_THROW`.
  - Added `CyberNeuron(int, int, Random)` and `CyberNeuron.of(int, int, Random)` so same-shape hidden neurons can break symmetry.
- `LearnStrategy` hardened and fixed:
  - `SEQUENTIAL` and `RANDOM` now redistribute remaining steps across unsaturated addressed rows instead of wasting steps on cells stuck at `Byte.MAX_VALUE` / `Byte.MIN_VALUE`, and stop immediately if all addressed rows are saturated.
  - Replaced shared `java.util.Random` with `ThreadLocalRandom.current()`, handled `Integer.MIN_VALUE` safely via `long`, and enforced lock/symbol bounds before writing.
- `CyberNetwork` teaching hardened and fixed:
  - Evaluated candidate confidence symbols across all consumers (including already-satisfied consumers so shared detectors do not break satisfied heads), grouped multi-slot links per `(source, target)` pair, and rejected opposing confidence transitions unless backed by genuinely inhibitory (`< 0`) cells to prevent `0 <-> 1` bootstrap ping-pong.
  - Separated hidden `edits` (bounded by downstream error-share magnitude so negative fan-in patterns do not wipe out shared detectors) from upstream `pressures` (propagating toward interior band centers `44` / `83` for `MAY_BE_NO` / `MAY_BE_YES` to preserve margin without overshooting).
  - Deterministic `LinkedHashMap` topological ordering, single-pass reverse topological `depths()`, and upfront lock/expected-band validation before mutating any neuron.
- Full verification (`mvn22 verify`: Enforcer, Checkstyle, 36 JUnit tests, SpotBugs) passes with zero warnings/bugs, covering single-neuron rules, 8-layer NOT, 4-input door rule, 3-input OR, 2-of-3 majority vote, simultaneous OR+AND on shared hidden detectors, 2-layer XOR, 4-bit OR-of-ANDs, `learningDivider = 4`, multi-slot links, and 8x8 digit recognition with all 20 held-out probes.

## 2. Architecture & Technical Discoveries
- Output is `sum_i sbox_i[input_i]`. There is no activation function and no weight multiplication; a single neuron is an additive lookup model over discrete symbols.
- Authors explicitly reject numeric backpropagation across cyberneuron layers (arXiv `0907.0229`): table indexing has zero slope inside a cell and step discontinuities at band thresholds (`25`, `63`, `102`).
- Between neurons a wire carries `signal()`, the `ConfidenceDegree` ordinal in `0..3`.
- Bounded interior bands (`MAY_BE_NO` `26..63`, `MAY_BE_YES` `64..102`) frequently act as hidden relay codes on randomly initialized tables (`CyberNeuron.of`); aiming upstream positive pressure at the band center (`44` / `83`) gives symmetric noise margin without overshooting into adjacent bands.
- `LearnStrategy.SEQUENTIAL` deterministically starts at `abs(modifier) % inputSize`; when `learningDivider` produces repeated sub-row (`1`-step) nudges, `LearnStrategy.RANDOM` avoids deterministic row-parity limit cycles.

## 5. Known Issues & Limitations
- A single `CyberNeuron` with separate binary inputs cannot represent XOR or `(a AND b) OR (c AND d)` for any table contents; solving them requires either multi-bit packed symbols or hidden neurons (`CyberNeuron.of(..., random)` for symmetric XOR inputs, or pair detectors for OR-of-ANDs).
- Pair tables alone cannot solve 3-bit parity because every 2-bit projection is balanced against the third bit; a 3-bit joint index or wider hidden structure is required.
- Quantizing wires to `0..3` limits inter-layer alphabet width; keep wide alphabets on input-layer tables for high pattern-memory capacity.
- Output neurons are trained only to caller-supplied bands and should be terminal nodes in `CyberNetwork`.
